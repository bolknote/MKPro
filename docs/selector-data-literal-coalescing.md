# Joint selector and data literal coalescing

## Independent companion address bounds

Each movable preloaded selector keeps its own two-digit placement bound.
An atomic literal erasure may temporarily widen a runtime charge's placement
domain, but that never grants the same extra addresses to its companions.
The geometry cache includes each bound, and final placement checks it again.

Erasing a literal can also shift a companion's target. The complete transaction
may rebind that preload only through the ordinary stable-selector relocation
proof, including non-flow data uses, memory aliases and certified fractional
projections. Every call keeps its command identity and return context. A stale
preload, unknown alias or unproved data change rejects the transaction; the
original artifact remains an independent candidate.

Charge discovery also follows an authoritative immutable indirect call or
tail jump into the shared store. Shortening the transfer must not destroy
the provenance of its adjacent numeric argument. The entry/data/stack proof
is still required for every mode; the opcode spelling alone is no proof.

## Coupled anchor release

The search retains the ordinary artifact as an independent candidate. When
an immutable selector pins a straight-line interval across the required data
address, a bounded alternative expands its complete ordinary indirect-call
and tail-jump family into direct transfers. The selector's delivered value and writeback must
be proved; runtime writes, memory aliases, noncanonical entries, unknown
targets and differing stack/X2 effects reject the alternative.

The existing direct-to-indirect command relocation proof runs in reverse to
prove every added operand and call/return context. Explicit deferred selector
contracts preserve unrelated anchored addresses while the layout solver
repacks components. The data literal remains present until final placement
and selector rebinding are proved, and only then is it coalesced. There are
at most eight conflicting-family attempts, with at most 512 geometry states
and 32 anchor combinations per attempt.
Both the ordinary placement and its size-neutral alternative survive until
the pending erasure and final entry proofs have run. Two erasure preimages
and two placement policies are considered per attempt; at most eight closed
alternatives enter the downstream compiler frontier.

Unchanged selectors retain their existing flexible proofs rather than becoming
absolute anchors. A shifted selector first tries the ordinary stable-preload
rebinder; only a non-retunable target needs a deferred fixed-address contract.

No expanded intermediate is published. The frontier may retain a neutral
transaction or at most two cells of debt when a direct jump provides a
possible command/operand repayment. Downstream helper merging and repayment
must prove and pay that debt; the
compiler accepts it only if that complete downstream artifact is strictly
smaller than its untouched incumbent. Reports account for the added calls
as well as the removed literal. Nothing depends on a game or procedure name.

A compiler-owned shared region can accept a procedure address in X, store
it in a private selector, and then load an ordinary literal as a stack input.
One entry mode needs that literal; the others independently discard it.
Instead of optimizing those instructions separately, place the consuming
procedure at the literal's numeric value and reuse its address as the input.

This is a constrained materialization/layout transaction, not source-name or
formula recognition. Discovery uses the existing sunk-literal provenance.
The original literal stays present while the ordinary natural-target solver
proves placement and preload data projections. The solver considers two
preimages: the literal's address itself and that address plus the width of
the pending erasure. Runtime selector binding is deferred until after the
erasure, so a provisional address 100 may legally become final address 98.
The provisional code is never executable output. All entry modes re-prove stack, physical
last-X, hidden X2, and exact call/return-context convergence on the final code.
Every subsequent final-artifact check re-derives the source address/value
equality rather than trusting the new role marker.

Private selector registers may not have ordinary data reads or indirect
memory aliases. Unknown, raw, executable-address-word, and formal side-space
operands fail closed. Ordinary numeric targets become command labels before
movement. An erasure may move the private runtime selector's targets only
when final rebinding and every entry's data/stack proof succeed. Moving a
companion destination also requires a proved stable-preload rebind. All
indirect destinations, external entries and return frames retain their
command identities. An unproved relocation rejects the transaction.

The search considers at most eight closed families, two erasure preimages
per family and at most 512 geometry states per preimage. A placement may temporarily spend the width of the literal,
but only a strictly smaller complete artifact can replace the incumbent.
All padding and selector costs count. Existing layouts remain competitors;
an impossible or expensive placement is not an error and changes nothing.
